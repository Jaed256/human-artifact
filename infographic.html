The Great Acceleration

AI coding assistants adoption went from novelty to default in about two years. The industry's own numbers show what actually sped up — and what didn't.

By Jaed Pizarro · IDH3034 · Synaptic Commits
01 — Adoption

AI tools stopped being optional

Across three independent industry surveys, AI-assisted coding crossed from "some developers" to "most developers" in a single reporting cycle.

84%

02 — Productivity vs. Trust

Faster, and less trusted, at the same time

The same developers reporting productivity gains are also reporting the lowest trust in AI accuracy Stack Overflow has recorded.

Sources: DORA 2025 (productivity, code quality) · Stack Overflow 2025 (trust, favorability)
DORA's own framing: AI is "an amplifier, not a fixer" — it magnifies whatever strengths or weaknesses a team's engineering practices already have.
03 — The Real Bottleneck

Typing was never the slow part

Code generation sped up dramatically. Maintenance, review, and debugging did not keep pace — and DORA finds AI adoption is now negatively associated with delivery stability, even as throughput rises.

Sources: Stack Overflow 2025 Developer Survey · DORA 2025 (throughput vs. stability relationship)
04 — Where This Goes

From autocomplete to autonomous agents

GitHub's Copilot coding agent already opened more than 1 million pull requests between May and September 2025. The next step isn't a smarter autocomplete — it's agents that clone a repo, write tests, fix a broken pipeline, and open the PR themselves.

Best case

Developer friction largely disappears. Engineers spend their time on system architecture, security, and human-centered design instead of boilerplate and repetitive fixes.

Worst case

Fragile, AI-generated technical debt and security vulnerabilities accumulate in production codebases faster than humans — or the same AI tools — can audit them.
